## What this means for a commercial post

- **Public domain / CC0** — use freely, no credit required.
- **CC BY** — commercial use is fine; you must credit the author.
- **CC BY-SA** — commercial use is fine and you must credit, but share-alike can be read as
  requiring the derivative work to carry the same licence. For a brand ad that is a real
  consideration, not a formality.

Three plates are share-alike: Rongorongo, Linear A and the Indus seal. Two clean ways out
if you'd rather not carry that licence:

1. Put the credits in the caption or a pinned comment (what most creators do), or
2. Swap those three. Museum open-access programmes (the Met and the Cleveland Museum of Art
   both release CC0) hold comparable objects, and 19th-century excavation plates of all
   three scripts are public domain by age. Commons also holds public-domain Rongorongo
   line-drawings, though they top out around 750x993 — too small for a full-frame push.

The Voynich, the Phaistos Disc and the Easter Island plate are public domain.

## Note on the Rongorongo plate

The first cut used `roro_champ` (Musée Champollion, CC BY 3.0), which is licence-cleaner.
It was swapped for `roro_chile` because the Champollion tablet is reddish-brown with very
low-contrast incisions — under this edit's dark grade the glyphs turned to an unreadable
smear. The Chilean plate's carving is white against dark teal and reads instantly, which
matters more on a card whose whole job is showing you writing. `roro_champ` is still
downloaded by `fetch_assets.py` if you'd rather trade legibility for the cleaner licence.
